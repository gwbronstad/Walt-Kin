Nov. 3, 1963 Letter from Anne Whaling to Walton Bronstad

Apt 837, University Terrace 
Ann Arbor, MI 48104

3320 Daniels
Dallas, TX 75205

Dear Walton,


I’ve been wondering ever since I mailed that monstrously long letter whether any of it was clear. I was writing as fast as I could just pouring out what came to mind, and it may have been impossible to find the point in all those words. I am also sure that the effect was blurred by my uncertainty about the reasons for your thinking of coming back to Texas. Could you bear a few more words of summary, after some reflection?


(Marginalium 1) not, of course, to change in itself (see back of this page [last 1/5 th of the letter]). ( end marginalium 1)


The major thing is an unqualified ascent to all the misgivings and negative possibilities involved in a change mentioned in your letter, with a far stronger warning of my own based on things you could not Know (at least your tone suggests that you do not) as for the reaction, any older academic person would see giving up an NSF fellowship and coming back to Texas as a tragedy, whatever the reason it might be (and this of course covers the possibility of another sacrifice on Pat‘s account.) academic failure, as such, isn’t really possible they would think. (as I do) given all the test results and any understanding by Michigan of the background (which they certainly had when they admitted you, to judge from Melton‘s letters) although there would be difficulties at first inevitably. I’ve seen you weather setbacks and take criticism beautifully – it’s one of the things I have admired – and no one who has known you would ever believe that disaster would be the result of failure of nerve of this kind. There is illness, of course, (of both physical and mental kind), but with all the resources available there for help with both (the medical school is one of the most distinguished) you certainly cannot give up on that score without trying. As for the last possibility– the one really grave danger – I have always thought that the one hope was for someone to help Pat see the effect on you. If she knew that her demands (stated or implied in the dramatic projection of misery) meant tragedy for you, she would never let you sacrifice your chance to do useful distinguish work and to take the place in the world you should have. If you do not want to plead your own cause, or do not know how to show her, please try to find someone in the university counseling services who can talk to her, or if a student's wife can’t actually use the services, someone could tell you what to do. I know that intelligent help of this kind is probably the rarest thing in the world, but if there is any, I think it should be in a university of this kind. Please don’t consider making an irrevocable step backwards until you have tried everything. (And please remember that you will have to (consciously end with great effort!) sacrifice your taste for concealment and evasion and give them all the information they need – otherwise advice is worse than useless.)


One last thing you may not think of. I am assuming that since you didn’t find Dr David before school end, you have been too busy and too much depressed to try again. (unless of course he is away on leave or something) I’ll be so glad, if you will consent to send him a note explaining that you have had a difficult time and possibly were a little embarrassed about calling on him so late. Of course this wouldn’t be necessary, really, if you just tell him yourself – he is wonderfully understanding, and I know he would remember my letter quite clearly. Of all the people I know, he is possibly quickest to grasp and respond to a difficult personal situation – not in a sentimentally, sympathetic or professionally helpful way – he will seem more like an oddly curious or inquisitive child as he watches you at first, and he’s always rather detached – he is the last person to intrude in any way. But he would be someone to talk to a little.


Over and over again, as I’ve thought about this, I’ve come back to the damage done by isolation. The severe depression coincided every time I saw it, with the increased time spit at home with Pat. Now, of course, it’s peculiarly difficult because the neighbors are busy studying, and she has probably not made any organized effort to study by herself. Thus, to please her, you will cut yourself off from the people you could see and talk to a little and it is probably much like thesituation at Arlington. It is no wonder, given all these things, that you are depressed to the point of academic suicide.


If you can just realize that this is the most difficult stage for everybody. I simply have not known anyone who did not find the first semester of graduate school, terribly hard and uncertain. I well remember Mike’s long silence, then a strange bare letter in November that frightened me as much as you have. He had tried to work part-time (in a movie theater, at night) and it just stopped when he wrote. I never found out whether his heart condition sent him to the hospital or what happened – when I saw him again, after the battle had been won, he merely said that there had been a “terrific adjustment“ involved in beginning then now he and Carolyn love the Washington area and would like to stay there. (though of course they will go wherever opportunity presents itself. He is teaching there and finished his thesis – should get his degree in June) Carolyn‘s position was very similar to Pat's, but it has worked out very happily for her. When they were here last year (August 1962) they cut their visit in Fort Worth short and found that they were really eager to get back “home“.


If you will just hold on until Christmas, things will begin to clear up, I imagine. You may find that people there anticipated much of this (I took the offer of a summer job to be a move to bridge over the transition, for one thing) please do not mislead and confuse everyone by being too silent – more than is normal, and try to let yourself trust people a little more. Some are worth all of it, and as for the other others, a little trust should not do much harm. You will find friends.


(Marginalium 2) I’ll try to send Pat a note with this. Please take courage if I can think of something constructive I’ll write at once. You are never very far from my thoughts.


(Marginalium 3) Virginia (who lived upstairs) found the transition from the University of Texas grad school to Pennsylvania, very difficult – to “graduate work in current“ as she put it, and she was good enough student to get a Woodrow Wilson Fellowship.


I realize that I didn’t make clearly the point about the difference in a change from E. E. To psychology, and a change now from psych to medicine or teaching math and science (presumably at the high school level since any other requires a doctor degree) the first change involved feels completely different in kind – the difference in the sort of abilities involved is very marked, very obvious and well-known. They require different kinds of people, and so far from indicating instability anyone would say this change indicated good sense (and in Arlington) much courage. In fact, everyone who knows of it did say that, and thought of it as a very narrow escape.


A change now that does not have the same kind of justification in the nature of the subject matter is a very different thing. And any change as early as this, before you have consolidated your position and proved yourself there, would be very damaging. Would indicate either instability or inability to control a personal factor – either would be regarded as a serious weakness. The worst thing of all would be going back to Texas (any school) this would seem for one thing, an attempt to return to the world of childhood (or high school) and purely academic terms, given the nature of Texas schools, incredible. (That is why I asked you not to tell Phillips and McCain you had thought of that.)


I am putting it rather crudely just as people would certainly put it, regardless of possible special considerations, because this is the way it would work, academically. Of course change later, after you completed a year there, and if it does not involve any move backwards that could not possibly be explained, would be different and even desirable - would indicate resource and intellectual independence.

Letter's Context

2026-11-26

Concenting the relation between two people, one can imagine a boundary between benevolent influence and exploitative manipulation. Imagine, yes, but I do not believe it exists or even is possible. In binary relations the balance scale of benevolence and manipulation may be pinned to one side , or may oscillate from side to side as the relationship evolves, as the situation changes. This letter reaches into tripartate relations.


There were long standing elements of tension between Ann and my wife Patricia. Anne's letter was addressed to both of us, and Pat read phrases like: "…that her (Pat's) demands (stated or implied in the dramatic projection of misery) meant tragedy for you," and " …the damage done by isolation. The severe depression coincided every time I saw it, with the increased time spit at home with Pat."


Pat was deeply hurt and enlisted neighbors Ros and Dick Zelman to read Anne's letter. They, of course, were supportive of Pat. Adding marital discord to academic discord was wholly unhelpful for me. It is hard to avoid the interpretation that Anne was more concerned abut her reputation, her professional standing (which she often seemed to regard as tenuous) than the people she was trying to steer.


The letter added to the strains in the relationship between Anne and me.
